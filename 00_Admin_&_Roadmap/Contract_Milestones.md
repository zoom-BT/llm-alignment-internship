## **WEEK 3 : ALIGNMENT, PREFERENCE LEARNING, AND EFFICIENT FINE-TUNING**

During Week 3, the Intern shall study the main methods used to adapt language models so that they better follow instructions, human preferences, safety requirements, and task-specific objectives.

The Intern shall learn how these methods differ according to:

* the type of feedback used;  
* whether a reward model is required;  
* whether training uses supervised learning or reinforcement learning;  
* whether feedback is collected from humans, AI systems, written rules, or automatic verifiers;  
* whether the full model or only a small number of parameters are updated.

The Intern is not expected to implement every method listed below. The objective is to understand the main families of approaches and experimentally evaluate a representative subset.

### **1\. Sources of Alignment Feedback**

The Intern shall study the main forms of feedback used during language-model post-training:

* **Demonstrations:** examples of instructions paired with high-quality expected responses;  
* **Pairwise preferences:** a prompt with a preferred response and a rejected response;  
* **Binary feedback:** responses independently labeled as desirable or undesirable;  
* **Rankings or numerical scores:** several responses ordered or scored according to quality;  
* **Natural-language critiques:** explanations of the errors in a response followed by a revised answer;  
* **Human feedback:** judgments provided by human annotators;  
* **AI feedback:** preferences, critiques, or scores produced by another language model;  
* **Rule-based feedback:** evaluation according to an explicit set of principles or safety rules;  
* **Verifiable rewards:** automatic feedback obtained from unit tests, exact answers, mathematical checks, formatting rules, or other programmatic verifiers.

The Intern shall understand that the quality of an alignment method strongly depends on the quality, diversity, and representativeness of its feedback.

### **2\. Supervised Alignment Methods**

The Intern shall study:

#### **Supervised Fine-Tuning**

Supervised Fine-Tuning, or SFT, trains a model on examples of desirable responses.

The Intern shall understand:

* instruction-response datasets;  
* conversational templates;  
* loss masking;  
* data quality and diversity;  
* the limitations of learning only from positive demonstrations.

#### **Rejection Sampling and Reward-Ranked Fine-Tuning**

These methods generate several candidate responses, select the best ones using a human evaluator, reward model, AI judge, or automatic verifier, and fine-tune the model on the selected responses.

The Intern shall understand the difference between:

* selecting a better response at inference time;  
* adding selected responses to a new training dataset;  
* directly optimizing the model against a reward.

### **3\. Reward Models and Evaluators**

The Intern shall study how a reward model or evaluator assigns a score to a response.

This includes:

* pairwise reward models trained from preferred and rejected responses;  
* outcome reward models that evaluate the final answer;  
* process reward models that evaluate intermediate reasoning steps;  
* rule-based verifiers;  
* language models used as judges.

The Intern shall understand common evaluator limitations, including:

* position bias;  
* preference for longer responses;  
* inconsistency;  
* cultural or linguistic bias;  
* disagreement with human judgments;  
* reward hacking.

### **4\. Reinforcement Learning Methods**

The Intern shall study the main principles of reinforcement learning for language-model alignment:

* the language model as a policy;  
* generated responses as trajectories;  
* rewards;  
* policy gradients;  
* reference models;  
* KL-divergence regularization;  
* reward normalization;  
* online generation and optimization.

The main methods to study shall include:

* **RLHF:** a general pipeline combining human preference data, reward modeling, and policy optimization;  
* **PPO:** an actor–critic algorithm commonly used in classical RLHF systems;  
* **REINFORCE:** a simpler policy-gradient method;  
* **RLOO:** a critic-free method that uses other sampled responses as baselines;  
* **GRPO:** a method that compares several responses generated for the same prompt and computes relative advantages within the group;  
* **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards:** reinforcement learning using automatically checkable rewards, particularly for mathematics, code, structured outputs, or tool-use tasks.

The Intern shall understand that RLHF is a general training framework, while PPO, RLOO, and GRPO are possible optimization algorithms within such frameworks.

### **5\. Direct Preference-Optimization Methods**

The Intern shall study methods that learn directly from preference data without requiring the complete reward-model-plus-reinforcement-learning pipeline.

The main methods shall include:

* **DPO:** learns from paired preferred and rejected responses using a reference model;  
* **IPO:** modifies the preference objective to reduce some forms of preference overfitting;  
* **KTO:** learns from independently labeled desirable and undesirable responses and does not require paired examples;  
* **ORPO:** combines supervised instruction tuning and preference optimization in a single training objective;  
* **SimPO:** uses a reference-free preference objective based on normalized response likelihoods;  
* **SLiC-HF:** calibrates sequence likelihoods using human preference data.

For each method, the Intern shall identify:

* the required dataset format;  
* whether a reference model is required;  
* whether an explicit reward model is required;  
* whether the training data are fixed or generated during training;  
* the main advantages;  
* the main limitations;  
* the expected computing cost.

### **6\. AI Feedback and Rule-Based Alignment**

The Intern shall study:

* **Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback**, where an AI model generates preferences or scores;  
* **Constitutional AI**, where responses are critiqued and revised according to a written set of principles;  
* **self-critique and revision**, where a model evaluates and improves its own response;  
* the use of AI systems to generate synthetic instruction, preference, and evaluation datasets.

The Intern shall pay particular attention to the risk that an AI evaluator reproduces its own factual errors, biases, cultural assumptions, or language limitations.

### **7\. Full and Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning**

The Intern shall understand that the alignment objective and the method used to update the model parameters are separate choices.

The following adaptation methods shall be studied:

* **Full fine-tuning:** updating all or most model parameters;  
* **Adapters:** inserting small trainable modules into a frozen model;  
* **Prefix tuning and prompt tuning:** optimizing trainable continuous prompts or prefixes;  
* **LoRA:** learning low-rank updates while keeping the original model frozen;  
* **QLoRA:** applying LoRA while storing the base model in a quantized representation.

The Intern shall compare these approaches according to:

* number of trainable parameters;  
* GPU-memory requirements;  
* training time;  
* storage cost;  
* performance;  
* ease of deployment;  
* risk of forgetting previously learned capabilities.

### **8\. Alignment Evaluation and Failure Modes**

The Intern shall study the main risks associated with alignment training:

* reward hacking;  
* reward overoptimization;  
* excessive refusal of harmless requests;  
* insufficient refusal of harmful requests;  
* sycophancy;  
* loss of response diversity;  
* preference overfitting;  
* catastrophic forgetting;  
* degradation of useful capabilities;  
* data contamination;  
* differences in behavior across languages and cultural contexts.

Special attention shall be given to whether methods trained mainly with English-language data transfer correctly to French, African languages, code-switched prompts, and African social or cultural contexts.

### **Practical Work**

6. compare at least two parameter-update methods, such as full fine-tuning, LoRA, and QLoRA;  
7. analyze improvements, regressions, response lengths, refusals, and failure cases.
