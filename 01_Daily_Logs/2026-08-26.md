## Concepts Covered Today (In English)
### Separating a Research Journal from a Codebase, Then Catching Up the Data Pipeline
- **Why code and journal don't have to live in the same repository:** the vault (notes, logs, proposals) documents *why* and *what was decided*; a separate code repository documents *how it runs* — mixing them makes both harder to navigate as the project grows past a single-week experiment.
- **Carrying over tested infrastructure deliberately, not wholesale:** copying only the generic, already-debugged pieces (quantization config, warmup-step conversion, checkpoint cleanup) into the new repository, while leaving Week 1-3's task-specific code (SFT dataset formatting, ORPO) behind — reuse without dragging along what doesn't apply.
- **A datasheet as a research artifact, not bureaucracy:** license, source, size, and construction method written down once so no later step has to re-derive or guess it.
- **Contamination vs. intentional overlap:** the general rule ("train and test must never share content") applies to UbuntuGuard's own train/test split, but not to Native-DPO vs. Translated-DPO sharing English source text — that overlap is the point of the H1 comparison, and needs arguing explicitly rather than assumed obvious.
- **Catching up a schedule honestly:** compressing three theoretical days of work into one real day is a legitimate response to a two-day slip (finalizing and presenting the proposal was real, necessary work) — the daily log records what actually happened each day, while the checklist records the theoretical calendar the contract expects.
