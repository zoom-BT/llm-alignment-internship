## Concepts Covered Today (In English)
### The DPO Family: Learning Preferences Without a Separate RL Loop
- **DPO's core trick:** it shows mathematically that the RLHF objective (reward model + PPO + KL penalty) has a *closed-form* optimal policy, which means you can skip training a separate reward model entirely and just do supervised-style training directly on (chosen, rejected) pairs against a frozen reference model — much simpler and more stable than PPO.
- **KTO's distinction from DPO:** DPO needs *paired* comparisons (this response beats that one, same prompt); KTO only needs each response labeled independently as good or bad — much easier data to collect, since you don't need two candidates for every prompt.
- **ORPO's simplification:** removes the reference model entirely, folding preference optimization directly into the SFT loss — one training run instead of two stages (SFT then DPO), the cheapest of the family to run.
