## Concepts Covered Today (In English)
### Knowing When to Restart Rather Than Repair
- **Nine deviations is a signal, not a setback.** Week 5 spent itself correcting a research design against reality — the missing training split, the wrong axis, the mis-specified metric, the wrong HHH orientation. Each correction was right in isolation; together they meant the design had been built before the field was understood.
- **The order matters more than the effort.** The v1 topic was written first and confronted with the literature afterwards. Reading the systematic review first, and choosing inside what it declares open, inverts that order — and is what a research proposal is supposed to do.
- **A deliberate pause is not lost time.** Continuing to patch would have consumed Week 6 as Week 5 was consumed, and produced a paper whose title no longer matched its claim.

### Reading a Systematic Review for What It Omits
- **A review's value is as much in its silences as in its findings.** Its "future work" section is a list of unclaimed ground, and its Discussion states which methods it does *not* cover. This one says continued pre-training is absent as a method, and calls for validating PEFT and cross-lingual transfer specifically in African languages.
- **Quoting verbatim before paraphrasing.** Each of the eight gaps was extracted as an exact quotation first, then reformulated. The reformulation is where interpretation creeps in; keeping the original alongside makes that interpretation checkable.
- **Not filtering by feasibility at extraction time.** Filtering early is precisely what produced a topic that had to be corrected nine times. The crossing with our data and compute is a separate, later step.
